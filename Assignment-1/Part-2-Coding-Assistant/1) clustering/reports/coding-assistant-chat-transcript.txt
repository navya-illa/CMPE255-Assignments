Current evidence: No temporal monitoring data exists.
Requires production or longitudinal data: Baselines and alert thresholds must be established using multiple historical periods rather than invented from this one dataset.
Retraining criteria
Retraining should be triggered by documented evidence, not solely by a fixed calendar.
Potential triggers include:


persistent feature-distribution drift;


substantial changes in cluster proportions;


increased distances from assigned centroids;


declining assignment stability;


appearance of new customer populations;


changes to the spending-score definition;


addition of transaction or RFM features;


deterioration in controlled campaign outcomes;


unacceptable demographic outcome disparities;


operational changes such as new malls or customer channels.


Exact thresholds cannot be supported by the current experiment. They should be established after collecting baseline distributions across multiple periods.
Every retraining cycle should:


repeat schema and data-quality validation;


refit preprocessing rather than reuse outdated scaling parameters;


reevaluate the number of clusters;


rerun stability analysis;


compare new and previous profiles;


evaluate fairness and business outcomes;


retain the previous model for rollback.


Retraining should not automatically replace the deployed model. The candidate must pass predefined acceptance criteria.
Measuring business value
Business value requires controlled experiments.
A suitable design would:


define a specific treatment for selected clusters;


randomly assign eligible customers within each cluster to treatment and control groups;


keep all other campaign conditions consistent;


define outcomes before examining results;


measure incremental effects rather than raw response rates;


report confidence intervals and practical effect sizes;


monitor adverse outcomes and subgroup disparities.


Possible outcomes include:


incremental conversion;


incremental revenue or margin;


redemption rate;


retention;


opt-outs;


complaints;


campaign cost per incremental outcome.


A useful comparison would test:


cluster-specific treatment;


a common non-segmented treatment;


no-treatment or business-as-usual control.


This would determine whether segmentation adds value beyond running a campaign without clusters.
Final readiness decision
Supported now: A reproducible academic analysis showing that K-Means k=6 provides an interpretable, full-coverage partition of the 200 supplied records and performs modestly better than the comparable Ward solution on the reported internal metrics.
Not supported now: Claims that the model generalizes, remains stable, improves campaigns, increases revenue, treats demographic groups fairly, or represents durable customer types.
The appropriate status is:

Exploratory model suitable for stability testing and a controlled pilot—not ready for production-wide business deployment.

Production consideration should require, at minimum:


a larger and independently collected sample;


temporal and cross-location validation;


completed cluster-stability analysis;


transaction and RFM evaluation;


documented sensitive-attribute governance;


drift baselines and rollback procedures;


evidence of incremental business value from controlled experiments.
